    <header>
      <h2>Aim and objectives</h2>
      <p>Purpose of the study and the research questions addressed.</p>
    </header>
    <div class="content">
      <h3>Aim</h3>
      <p>To map and quantify burn severity in <span class="areaName">the study area</span> using Sentinel-2 surface reflectance and the differenced Normalized Burn Ratio (dNBR), and to assess early vegetation recovery one year after the fire using the change in NDVI, with all processing done in Google Earth Engine.</p>
      <h3>Objectives</h3>
      <ol>
        <li>Build cloud-, shadow- and water-masked Sentinel-2 median composites for the pre-fire, immediate post-fire and one-year recovery periods.</li>
        <li>Compute NBR for the pre- and post-fire periods and difference them to produce a continuous burn severity surface (dNBR).</li>
        <li>Classify dNBR into burn severity classes and report the area (hectares) of each class.</li>
        <li>Compute NDVI for the post-fire and one-year periods, difference it to measure regrowth, and classify the recovery.</li>
        <li>Interpret the spatial and statistical patterns, state the uncertainties, and recommend how to validate the maps.</li>
      </ol>
      <h3>Research questions</h3>
      <ul>
        <li>What proportion of the study area exhibits a measurable decline in vegetation condition, and what share of it is of moderate-high or high severity?</li>
        <li>How is first-year vegetation recovery distributed across the burned landscape?</li>
        <li>To what extent is vegetation recovery associated with burn severity?</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
  </section>

          <span class="tag used">Used</span>
          <h3>Sentinel-2 MSI Level-2A, harmonized</h3>
          <code>COPERNICUS/S2_SR_HARMONIZED</code>
          <p>Atmospherically corrected surface reflectance from the Copernicus Sentinel-2 mission (ESA), 10, 20 and 60 m bands, revisit of about five days with two satellites. Reflectance is stored scaled by 10,000. The harmonized collection corrects the offset introduced by processing baseline 04.00, so scenes from before and after early 2022 are comparable.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="ds">
          <span class="tag used">Used</span>
          <h3>Area of interest (AOI)</h3>
          <code>projects/shruti-ee/assets/Polygons</code>
          <p>The study area boundary, stored as an Earth Engine feature collection. All composites, statistics and area totals are confined to this boundary.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="ds">
          <span class="tag used">Used</span>
          <h3>Scene Classification Layer (SCL)</h3>
          <code>SCL band, 20 m</code>
          <p>A per-pixel class map shipped with Level-2A products. It is used for the pixel-level mask: saturated, cloud shadow, water, unclassified, medium and high cloud probability, cirrus and snow are removed. Dark-area pixels are kept because burn scars are dark.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="ds">
          <span class="tag">Recommended for validation</span>
          <h3>Reference data</h3>
          <code>fire perimeters, field plots, high-resolution imagery</code>
          <p>Official fire perimeters, Composite Burn Index (CBI) field plots, or very-high-resolution imagery give an independent check on the severity map. MODIS or VIIRS active-fire detections help confirm the fire dates.</p>
        </div>
      </div>

  <section class="doc" id="method">
    <header>
      <h2>Method</h2>
      <p>Processing workflow, spectral indices and classification schemes.</p>
    </header>
    <div class="content">
      <p>The workflow comprises seven stages. Select a stage for a description.</p>
      <div class="steps" role="tablist" id="stepTabs"></div>
      <div class="steppanel" id="stepPanel" role="tabpanel" aria-live="polite"></div>

      <h3>Indices</h3>
      <div class="formula">NBR = (B8A − B12) / (B8A + B12)</div>
      <div class="formula">dNBR = NBR<sub>pre-fire</sub> − NBR<sub>post-fire</sub></div>

      <h3>Guide to the charts</h3>
      <details open><summary>Burn severity bar chart</summary><div>
        <p>A concentration of area in the moderate-high and high classes indicates extensive canopy consumption and exposure of char and bare soil. A concentration in the lower classes indicates light or patchy burning. The increased-greenness and unburned classes do not represent fire effects.</p>
      </div></details>
      <details><summary>Severity share doughnut</summary><div>
        <p>Presents the severity distribution as proportions, enabling comparison with other fires or periods irrespective of area. The low to high severity classes together approximate the burned fraction.</p>
      </div></details>
      <details><summary>Regrowth bar chart</summary><div>
        <p>A distribution weighted towards the lower classes indicates slow first-year recovery, while one weighted towards the moderate and high classes indicates rapid regrowth, typically dominated by grasses, herbs and resprouting shrubs rather than restored forest canopy. Areas of NDVI decline may reflect delayed mortality, seasonal differences or residual cloud.</p>
      </div></details>
      <details><summary>NDVI trajectory</summary><div>
        <p>A healthy stand shows high NDVI before the fire, a sharp decline afterwards and a gradual increase during recovery. The recovery fraction is defined as (one year − post-fire) ÷ (pre-fire − post-fire); a value close to 1 denotes a return to pre-fire greenness. Herbaceous regrowth can raise NDVI rapidly without restoring forest structure.</p>
      </div></details>
      <details><summary>Relating severity to regrowth</summary><div>
        <p>High-severity patches frequently exhibit the largest absolute NDVI gain because they begin from the lowest values, whereas unburned and low-severity areas change little. A formal assessment of this relationship requires cross-tabulation of the two class maps or sampling of both rasters at random points.</p>
      </div></details>
    </div>

  <!-- LIMITATIONS -->
  <section class="doc" id="limits">
    <header>
      <h2>Limitations and validation</h2>
      <p>Sources of uncertainty and recommended validation.</p>
    </header>
    <div class="content">
      <details open><summary>Seasonal and phenology effects</summary><div>
        <p>Where the pre-fire and post-fire periods fall in different phenological stages, dNBR incorporates seasonal change in addition to fire effects. The recovery period (July to September 2021) is also longer than the single-month post-fire period, which may introduce additional seasonal variation.</p>
      </div></details>
      <details><summary>Clouds, smoke and shadow</summary><div>
        <p>The SCL mask removes most cloud, but thin smoke and haze may persist and bias NBR, particularly immediately after a fire. Median compositing reduces, but does not eliminate, this effect. Composites built from only one or two scenes are less reliable.</p>
      </div></details>
      <details><summary>Threshold choice</summary><div>
        <p>Class limits are conventions. The custom limits differ from the USGS scheme, and appropriate thresholds vary between ecosystems. Absolute dNBR also depends on pre-fire biomass; relative indices such as RdNBR (Miller and Thode, 2007) and RBR perform better in heterogeneous landscapes.</p>
      </div></details>
      <details><summary>Resolution and area accuracy</summary><div>
        <p>NBR is computed at 20 m, so each pixel represents 0.04 ha and small or narrow burn patches are generalised. Area statistics were computed with <code>bestEffort</code>, which may coarsen the scale over very large areas; exact totals require export and offline computation of the class rasters.</p>
      </div></details>
      <details><summary>NDVI saturation and what "regrowth" means</summary><div>
        <p>NDVI saturates in dense canopy and is dominated by herbaceous cover in the early post-fire stage. A large first-year ΔNDVI therefore does not imply forest recovery. Conclusions on ecosystem recovery require structural or multi-year observations.</p>
      </div></details>
      <details><summary>Water masking, terrain and other land cover</summary><div>
        <p>Water was masked as it does not constitute fuel. Terrain shading, agricultural activity and harvesting can also alter dNBR and NDVI and be misattributed to fire or recovery. Masking such areas with a land-cover dataset is recommended.</p>
      </div></details>
      <details open><summary>Recommended validation</summary><div>
        <ul>
          <li>Comparison of the mapped burned extent with official fire perimeters, reporting spatial overlap (for example, the Dice or Jaccard coefficient).</li>
          <li>Accuracy assessment against Composite Burn Index plots or visually interpreted high-resolution reference points, reporting overall, producer's and user's accuracy and the kappa coefficient.</li>
          <li>Sensitivity analysis of the cloud threshold, compositing periods and class limits.</li>
        </ul>
      </div></details>
    </div>
  </section>

var lastText='';
function interpret(){
  var sev=SEV_HA,reg=REG_HA;
  var tot=sum(sev),regTot=sum(reg);
  var box=$('#interpBox'),paras=[],plain=[];
  function P(html,flag){paras.push('<p'+(flag?' class="flag"':'')+'>'+html+'</p>');plain.push(html.replace(/<[^>]+>/g,''));}

  if(tot<=0&&regTot<=0){
    box.innerHTML='<p>Findings will be presented once the class areas are available.</p>';
    lastText='';return;
  }
  var area=STUDY.areaName||'the study area';

  if(tot>0){
    var burned=sev[2]+sev[3]+sev[4]+sev[5];
    var mh=sev[4]+sev[5];
    var pctB=100*burned/tot;
    var pctMH=burned>0?100*mh/burned:0;
    var dom=2;for(var i=3;i<6;i++){if(sev[i]>sev[dom])dom=i;}
    P('Of the <strong>'+ha(tot)+' ha</strong> analysed in '+area+', <strong>'+ha(burned)+' ha ('+pctB.toFixed(1)+'%)</strong> fall in dNBR classes of 0 or higher, indicating a decline in NBR between the pre-fire and post-fire composites. The most extensive affected class is <strong>'+SEV_NAMES[dom].toLowerCase()+'</strong> ('+ha(sev[dom])+' ha).');
    if(burned>0){
      var txt;
      if(pctMH>=50) txt='More than half of the affected area ('+pctMH.toFixed(0)+'%) is in the moderate-high or high classes. This pattern is characteristic of fires that consumed most of the canopy and implies extensive patches of strong spectral change with a probable slow structural recovery.';
      else if(pctMH>=25) txt='A substantial share of the affected area ('+pctMH.toFixed(0)+'%) is moderate-high or high severity, alongside a larger area of lighter burning. This indicates a mixed-severity fire producing a mosaic of heavily and lightly burned stands.';
      else txt='Most of the affected area is in the low and low-moderate classes, with only '+pctMH.toFixed(0)+'% at moderate-high or high severity. This is consistent with a patchy or low-intensity burn, although dNBR alone cannot distinguish surface fire from crown fire.';
      P(txt);
    }
    var lowShare=burned>0?100*sev[2]/burned:0;
    if(lowShare>40) P('Because the custom low class starts at dNBR 0, and the USGS scheme treats values up to 0.10 as unburned, the low-severity area ('+ha(sev[2])+' ha) should be regarded as an upper estimate.',true);
    var g=100*sev[0]/tot;
    if(g>5) P(g.toFixed(1)+'% of the area has dNBR below −0.25 (increased greenness). This is not a fire effect and most likely reflects seasonal differences between the compositing periods, soil moisture or residual haze.',true);
  }

  if(regTot>0){
    var good=reg[3]+reg[4];
    var pg=100*good/regTot, pl=100*reg[2]/regTot, pn=100*(reg[0]+reg[1])/regTot, pd=100*reg[0]/regTot;
    var r;
    if(pg>=50) r='Early regrowth is strong: '+pg.toFixed(0)+'% of the area reached moderate or high recovery.';
    else if(pg>=25) r='Recovery is spatially uneven: '+pg.toFixed(0)+'% of the area reached moderate or high recovery, while '+pl.toFixed(0)+'% is only at the low class.';
    else r='Recovery after one year is limited: only '+pg.toFixed(0)+'% of the area reached moderate or high recovery, and '+pn.toFixed(0)+'% shows little or no gain or a decline.';
    P(r+' First-year NDVI gains are commonly driven by grasses, herbs and resprouting shrubs and should not be equated with forest canopy recovery.');
    if(pd>10) P(pd.toFixed(1)+'% of the area shows a negative ΔNDVI, which may indicate delayed mortality but may also arise from seasonal mismatch or residual cloud.',true);
  }

  var a=numOrNaN(STUDY.meanNdviPre),b=numOrNaN(STUDY.meanNdviPost),c=numOrNaN(STUDY.meanNdviOneYear);
  if(isFinite(b)&&isFinite(c)){
    var dd=c-b,t='Mean NDVI changed from '+b.toFixed(2)+' after the fire to '+c.toFixed(2)+' a year later (ΔNDVI '+(dd>=0?'+':'')+dd.toFixed(2)+')';
    if(isFinite(a)&&a>b){
      var rec=100*dd/(a-b);
      t+='. Pre-fire mean NDVI was '+a.toFixed(2)+', so about <strong>'+Math.max(0,rec).toFixed(0)+'%</strong> of the NDVI lost to the fire had returned';
      if(rec>100) t+=' (above 100% suggests a seasonal difference or a greener cover type than before)';
    }
    P(t+'.');
  }
  var dm=numOrNaN(STUDY.meanDnbr);
  if(isFinite(dm)){
    var cs=customSev(dm);
    P('The mean dNBR of '+dm.toFixed(2)+' falls in the <strong>'+cs.name.toLowerCase()+'</strong> class of the custom scheme and in the USGS class <strong>'+usgsSev(dm).toLowerCase()+'</strong>. As a mean conceals spatial variability, it should be interpreted together with the class areas.');
  }
  P('These findings describe spectral change between satellite composites rather than field-measured fire effects, and they have not yet been validated against independent reference data.',true);

  box.innerHTML=paras.join('');
  lastText=plain.join('\n\n');
}

(function(){
  var reduce=window.matchMedia&&window.matchMedia('(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)').matches;
  if(reduce) return;
  var hero=$('.hero h1');
  var heads=$$('section.doc > header h2');
  var all=(hero?[hero]:[]).concat(heads);
  if(!all.length) return;

  function build(el){
    var text=el.textContent;
    el.textContent='';
    var sr=document.createElement('span');sr.className='sr';sr.textContent=text;
    var wrap=document.createElement('span');wrap.setAttribute('aria-hidden','true');
    var idx=0;
    var words=text.split(' ');
    words.forEach(function(word,wi){
      var w=document.createElement('span');w.className='wd';
      for(var i=0;i<word.length;i++){
        var c=document.createElement('span');
        c.className='ch';c.textContent=word.charAt(i);
        c.style.setProperty('--i',idx++);
        w.appendChild(c);
      }
      wrap.appendChild(w);
      if(wi<words.length-1) wrap.appendChild(document.createTextNode(' '));
    });
    el.appendChild(sr);el.appendChild(wrap);
    el.classList.add('split','pending');
  }
  // Fix every letter to its final width so the changing weight never reflows the text.
  function lock(el){
    var chs=$$('.ch',el);
    chs.forEach(function(c){c.style.width='';});
    var widths=chs.map(function(c){return c.getBoundingClientRect().width;});
    chs.forEach(function(c,i){c.style.width=widths[i]+'px';});
  }
  function play(el){el.classList.remove('pending');el.classList.add('play');}

  all.forEach(build);

  var started=false;
  function start(){
    if(started) return;
    started=true;
    all.forEach(lock);
    if(hero) play(hero);
    if('IntersectionObserver' in window){
      var io=new IntersectionObserver(function(entries){
        entries.forEach(function(e){
          if(e.isIntersecting){play(e.target);io.unobserve(e.target);}
        });
      },{rootMargin:'0px 0px -10% 0px'});
      heads.forEach(function(h){io.observe(h);});
    }else{
      heads.forEach(play);
    }
  }
  var timer=setTimeout(start,2500);
  if(document.fonts&&document.fonts.ready){
    document.fonts.ready.then(function(){clearTimeout(timer);start();});
  }
  var rt;
  window.addEventListener('resize',function(){
    clearTimeout(rt);
    rt=setTimeout(function(){if(started) all.forEach(lock);},200);
  });
})();

})();
